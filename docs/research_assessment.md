# Research assessment

This session establishes a tested baseline, not a claimed new landscape result.
The exact topology, known example, finite-domain hierarchy and low-K count
comparison are reproduction work. Explicit rank and algebraic-witness artifacts
make parts of that reproduction auditable.

The first research question is now measurable: K=2,Gamma=2 on X7862 starts with
5^16 ordered four-column assignments (before deriving the fifth). The hierarchy
materializes 38,161 complete canonical bundles and retains 120 after cheap
necessary filters. After ambient row permutations there are five orbits.
Stage counters identify where work is spent; they do not supply an independent
formal partition of all nominal matrices.

The next algorithmic experiment should profile completion and pair-budget bounds
at K=2, then compare a proposed exact improvement against the independent
nonempty-domain tests and unchanged canonical survivor set. A larger-K run needs
an explicit cost estimate. Replayable branch-level rejection logs remain future
work; current independent replay is limited to affordable domains.

The cohomology backends deliberately avoid claiming general all-integer formulas.
The X7447 resultant reduction is an elementary specialization of relative Koszul
algebra. A systematic generalization could be useful, but novelty would require
comparison with existing computational and Hilbert-function literature and
independent map implementations. No new theorem is claimed here.

For physical model certification, priority goes to specifying the actual free
action, invariant defining-equation family, equivariant structures and cohomology
representations. Generic cohomology may jump on that family. Repeated line
bundles and additional integral relations also demand a complete commutant
analysis. Merely counting five summands does not resolve those issues.
